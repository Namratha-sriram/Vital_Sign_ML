4. Academic Viva Defense Guide (Faculty Questions & Answers)

QUESTION 01

Why did the model results improve after optimization?

The Baseline model was a simple linear classifier that assumes linear separation boundaries in log-odds space. It cannot capture non-linear physiological interactions (e.g., elevated glucose combined with acute hypoxia). The Optimized Random Forest aggregates 100 decorrelated decision trees using bagging (bootstrap aggregating), reducing variance and capturing complex non-linear clinical patterns.

QUESTION 02

Why is False Negative reduction the most critical metric?

In clinical medicine, a False Negative means an unstable deteriorating patient is mistakenly predicted as safe and discharged home, leading to severe complications or mortality. By cutting False Negatives by 22.6% (278 fewer missed patients), the optimized model significantly enhances clinical safety over raw accuracy alone.

QUESTION 03

Why was Z-score standardization applied to vitals?

Raw vital signs have drastically different measurement scales (e.g., Blood Glucose ranges up to 290 mg/dL, while SpO2 ranges 89–100%). Without feature normalization, features with larger ranges artificially dominate loss gradients and decision splits. Z-score scaling centers all continuous vitals to mean 0 and unit variance.
